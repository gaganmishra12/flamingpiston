BMW 7 Series vs Mercedes S-Class vs Genesis G90: The Flagship Sedan Fight

9 min read

Share

The flagship luxury sedan is a category where the argument should be simple: buy the best. The problem is that "best" means something different depending on who is sitting in the car. If it's the owner behind the wheel, the BMW 7 Series makes the most sense. If it's a passenger in the back, the Mercedes S-Class has been the answer for decades. If it's a buyer who wants both — and doesn't need the badge on the bonnet to justify the spend — the Genesis G90 has become a genuine option that most people haven't seriously considered.

Quick Verdict

Buy the Mercedes S-Class if you are buying a flagship and the name matters — to you, to your business, or to whoever is opening the door for you. It sets the benchmark for a reason, and the 2026 facelift's PHEV powertrain makes the case stronger than it's been in years. Buy the BMW 7 Series if you drive your own flagship. No car at this size engages a driver the way the 7 does, and the i7 electric variant is one of the best EVs in any category. Buy the Genesis G90 if product quality matters more to you than brand equity, and you want to spend significantly less for a result that will surprise most people who sit in it.

The Mercedes S-Class: The Default Benchmark

The S-Class has been defining what the upper end of the automotive market looks like since the 1970s. Features that first appeared on the S-Class — anti-lock brakes, electronic stability control, airbags, radar-guided cruise control — have since spread across the entire industry. That innovation pipeline continues in the 2026 facelift, which introduces 15 airbags as standard (more than any production car at launch), the MBUX Superscreen triple-display setup, and for the first time in India, a plug-in hybrid powertrain with 115 km of electric-only range. The rear cabin in long-wheelbase form — which is the only version sold in India and most Asian markets — reclines to 43.5 degrees and creates a genuinely private, aircraft-style experience for the passenger in the right-rear seat.

The S-Class has two structural advantages that no amount of product quality from competitors can easily overcome. The first is its role as the default status signal for business and diplomatic contexts globally — the S-Class communicates a specific kind of seriousness that the BMW 7 Series and Genesis G90, for all their quality, do not yet match. The second is its technology leadership: the 2026 facelift's MB.OS software platform is the most sophisticated in-car operating system currently in production, and it creates an interior experience that feels genuinely ahead of where both rivals currently sit.

The BMW 7 Series: For the Owner Who Drives

The 7 Series has never quite escaped the S-Class's shadow in the rear-passenger luxury conversation, and BMW has largely stopped trying. Instead, the current G70 generation leans harder into what BMW does better than anyone: building large cars that reward the person behind the wheel. The chassis is the most alive of the three flagships — it communicates road texture, weights up properly through corners, and doesn't feel like it's isolating you from the act of driving in the way the S-Class does. This is a feature for some buyers and a drawback for others. If you have a driver, you may never notice. If you drive yourself, you will notice immediately.

The i7 fully electric variant deserves special mention. BMW's electric powertrain in the i7 xDrive60 — 544 hp, 745 Nm, approximately 550 km WLTP range — is one of the most complete electric luxury sedans in production. The Theatre Screen for rear passengers (a 31.3-inch retractable display) is a feature that no rival currently offers and makes the i7's rear cabin experience genuinely cinematic. The 7 Series also offers the best driving dynamics of the three in any powertrain, including the plug-in hybrid 750e variant.

The Genesis G90: The One You Have to Drive to Understand

The G90 is the hardest of the three to evaluate from specification sheets because so much of its case rests on tactile and visual impression rather than headline numbers. The 27-inch OLED panoramic display is, on paper, the largest in-car screen of the three — but what matters is that it looks and functions better than screens of equivalent size in competitors, because Genesis's software team has calibrated the resolution and interface for this specific application rather than adapting an existing platform. The G90's rear cabin in the two-seat configuration rivals the best the Germans offer, with individual massage and recline seats and a cabin insulation level that makes the interior feel more private than the physical dimensions suggest.

Pricing is the G90's most striking characteristic relative to the S-Class and 7 Series. At comparable specification levels, the G90 costs significantly less — often by 20–30 percent — and includes features as standard that would be expensive options on the German rivals. The ownership package adds five years of complimentary servicing. The trade-off is the same as with the GV80: Genesis is newer to this market, residual values are less predictable, and the brand does not yet carry the context-signalling weight of the S-Class name in professional and diplomatic settings. These are real differences. They matter to some buyers and are completely irrelevant to others.

The Question Behind the Question

The real decision in this group isn't about which car is best — it's about what you're actually buying. If the purchase is partly about communicating something externally — status, success, seriousness — the S-Class is the only honest answer. It is the universal luxury vehicle signal in a way neither rival can claim. If the purchase is about the driving experience and you happen to need a large sedan rather than a large SUV, the 7 Series is the best driver's car in the segment and the i7 is one of the best electric vehicles in any class. If the purchase is purely about what you get for the money — the interior, the technology, the ownership cost, the product itself — the G90 makes a case that is very difficult to argue with, provided you are comfortable being the person who chose something most people haven't encountered before.

Buy the Mercedes S-Class if

  • The name and what it communicates matter in your professional or social context
  • You want the most advanced in-car software platform currently available
  • The 115 km PHEV range makes sense for your daily commute with home charging
  • Rear passenger experience is the primary brief — the LWB cabin is world-class

Skip the S-Class if

  • You drive yourself and want the most rewarding experience behind the wheel
  • You want the best value at this level — the G90 undercuts it significantly
  • A fully electric flagship is appealing — the i7 leads here

Buy the BMW 7 Series / i7 if

  • You drive your own car and want a flagship that rewards driver input
  • A fully electric luxury sedan appeals — the i7 is the benchmark EV flagship
  • The rear Theatre Screen and executive lounge seating are features you'll actually use

Skip the 7 Series if

  • Brand status signalling in traditional business contexts is important
  • You want the most technology for the money — the G90 and S-Class lead

Buy the Genesis G90 if

  • Product quality matters more to you than brand heritage
  • You want the most specification per pound/dollar/rupee at this level
  • The complimentary 5-year service package has real value in your ownership plan
  • You enjoy owning something that genuinely surprises people

Skip the Genesis G90 if

  • The car will be used in contexts where brand recognition is part of the point
  • Predictable resale value is important to your ownership model
  • You need a PHEV powertrain — the G90 currently doesn't offer one
