Kia Syros EV: Range, Price and Which Trim to Buy

Image: Kia India, edited by FlamingPiston
Gagan Mishra
Published Aug 5, 2026 · Updated Nov 21, 2026
8 min read Category: Electric SUVs
Share f 𝕏

Kia’s pitch for the Syros EV is a long range, fast charging and more tech than anything else at the price. Autocar India’s real-world test now puts a number on the range claim. The other question is how much of the tech you get if you don’t buy the top trim.

A Note on This Review

This review uses Kia India’s published specifications and prices and Autocar India’s drive and range test, not our own test. Prices and specifications were checked on 21 November 2026. See our Review Methodology for how we separate editorial assessment from measured testing.

Quick Verdict

The Syros EV is roomy, quick and efficient, and the 42 kWh versions undercut most rivals. The 51.4 kWh car is claimed at 526 km and returned about 400 km in Autocar India’s real-world test. Be careful at the top end: the 360-degree camera, driver-assistance systems and ambient lighting are reserved for the ₹20 lakh X-Line.

Seven trims, two batteries. The 42 kWh pack powers the HTK, HTK+ and HTX; the 51.4 kWh Extended Range (ER) pack powers the rest. Kia also offers Battery-as-a-Service, which drops the purchase price to ₹7.99 lakh and charges ₹3.3 per kilometre for the battery. That suits low-mileage buyers more than high-mileage ones.

The HTK+ is the sensible starting point. Choose ER only if you regularly drive long distances, and note that the X-Line costs only ₹50,000 more than the HTX+ ER but is the only trim with driver assistance and the 360-degree camera.

2

Exterior Design

The Syros EV keeps the boxy, upright shape of the petrol and diesel Syros, which looks more at home as an electric car. Kia’s Ice Cube LED headlamps, Star Map running lights and tail-lamps, flush door handles and three-spoke 17-inch alloys on higher trims set it apart. Autocar India liked the wheels. The tall, narrow body has a downside on the highway, which we cover in the range figures below.

Image: Kia India
3

Interior Design & Practicality

The cabin is the Syros’s strongest point. The 30-inch Trinity display combines the instruments, touchscreen and a 5-inch climate panel, and Autocar India rated the cabin spacious and practical. The rear bench slides and reclines on higher trims.

The boot holds 390 litres with the rear seat back, or 465 litres with it slid fully forward on trims that have the sliding seat. There is also a 16-litre frunk, though Autocar India found it too narrow to take the charging cable in its case.

Image: Kia India
4

Features & Safety

Every Syros EV gets six airbags, disc brakes on all four wheels, tyre-pressure monitoring and ISOFIX mounts. Higher trims add an eight-speaker Harman Kardon system, wireless charging, ventilated seats with power adjustment for the driver, and Kia Connect with over-the-air updates and remote functions. Every version has steering paddles for the regenerative braking.

The catch is the top trim. Autocar India confirms that the Level 2 driver-assistance suite (16 functions, including adaptive cruise with stop-and-go and lane-keep assist), the 360-degree camera, ambient lighting and the rear three-pin power socket are X-Line only. The X-Line comes only in Aurora Black Pearl or Matte Graphite. Kia’s specification sheet also lists the digital key and dual-pane sunroof with the top trim. Check the trim sheet for any feature you consider essential.

Image: Kia India
5

Mileage, Engine & Performance

Both batteries drive the front wheels through a single motor. Kia claims 0–100 km/h in 8.1 seconds for the 51.4 kWh car, which Autocar India could not verify but described as brisk.

BatteryPower / TorqueClaimed Range (MIDC)
42 kWh133 bhp (CarDekho)443 km
51.4 kWh (ER)171 PS / 255 Nm526 km

Autocar India found the regenerative braking well calibrated, with a one-pedal i-Pedal mode that is handy in town. The motor is quiet, so you hear more road noise. Kia claims a 10–80% DC charge in 39 minutes on a 100 kW charger, and offers a lifetime warranty on the high-voltage battery, subject to its terms.

The Syros EV is very efficient in town but much less so at highway speeds, where Autocar India blames the boxy shape. Plan for roughly 300 km between charges on long highway trips with the bigger battery.

Image: Kia India
6

Tech & Safety Deep-Dive

This section draws on Kia’s published specifications and Autocar India’s drive, not our own, in line with our Review Methodology. The Syros EV has not been crash-tested by Bharat NCAP. Its ratings list includes the petrol and diesel Syros for select variants, but not the EV. Its safety case therefore rests on its standard equipment, and on the driver-assistance suite if you buy the X-Line.

On the road, Autocar India found the ride a touch more absorbent than the petrol and diesel Syros, with an underlying firmness that keeps the tall body in check.

Image: Kia India

Why These Scores

Design: A distinctive shape that suits an electric car.

Performance: Brisk 51.4 kWh performance and well-judged regen.

Mileage Potential: Very efficient in town; about 400 km average in Autocar India’s test.

Comfort: A slightly firm ride and some road noise.

Features: A long equipment list, though the best items are X-Line only.

Safety: Good standard kit, but no Bharat NCAP rating yet.

Practicality: A roomy cabin, sliding rear seat and up to 465-litre boot.

Value for Money: The 42 kWh versions are good value; the top end is pricey.

7

Verdict

The Syros EV is one of the most practical and efficient electric SUVs at its price, and the 42 kWh HTK+ is the value pick for city drivers. The 51.4 kWh car’s 526 km claim becomes about 400 km in the real world, which is still competitive. Before paying for the X-Line, decide whether you need its driver assistance and 360-degree camera. No other trim has them. The missing crash-test rating is the one gap in its case.

Image: Kia India

Who Should Buy This

  • City drivers who want a roomy, efficient compact EV
  • Families who need a big boot and a flexible rear seat
  • Buyers who want to lower the upfront cost with Battery-as-a-Service
  • Tech-focused buyers willing to pay for the X-Line

Who Should Avoid This

  • Buyers who want a published crash-test rating
  • Drivers who do long highway runs and need more than about 300 km
  • Anyone who wants driver assistance below the top trim

FAQs

What is the price of the Kia Syros EV?
Kia India lists it from ₹13,49,900 (HTK, 42 kWh) to ₹19,99,900 (X-Line, 51.4 kWh), ex-showroom. Battery-as-a-Service starts at ₹7.99 lakh plus ₹3.3 per km.

What is the real-world range of the Syros EV?
Kia claims 526 km for the 51.4 kWh pack (MIDC). Autocar India’s test averaged 402 km: 489 km in the city and 316 km on the highway.

Which Syros EV trim has ADAS and a 360-degree camera?
Only the top X-Line, according to Autocar India and Kia’s specification sheet.

How fast does the Syros EV charge?
Kia claims 10–80% in 39 minutes on a 100 kW DC fast charger.

Does the Syros EV have a Bharat NCAP rating?
No. Bharat NCAP has rated the petrol and diesel Syros, but the EV is not on its list.

How big is the Syros EV’s boot?
390 litres, or 465 litres with the rear seat slid forward, plus a small 16-litre frunk.
