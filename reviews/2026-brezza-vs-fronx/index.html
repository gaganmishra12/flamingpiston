2026 Maruti Brezza vs Fronx: Which One Should You Buy?

Both of these are Maruti's own compact SUVs, sold through different showrooms, aimed at overlapping budgets, and cross-shopped constantly. Before price or features, there's one fact that decides this for a specific kind of buyer immediately: the automatic gearbox and the engine you want aren't paired together on either car, and it's the opposite pairing on each one.

The One Fact That Decides This For Some Buyers Immediately

The Brezza's real 6-speed torque-converter automatic is only available with the 1.5-litre naturally aspirated petrol, the 1.0 turbo is manual-only. The Fronx flips this exactly: its real 6-speed torque-converter automatic is only available with the 1.0-litre turbo-petrol, the 1.2 naturally aspirated engine gets a manual or an AMT, not a true automatic. Want turbo power with a proper self-shifting gearbox? Only the Fronx offers that combination. Want a relaxed, non-turbo automatic? Only the Brezza offers that. Neither car lets you have both ways.

The Fronx undercuts the Brezza by roughly ₹55,000 at the entry point and by close to ₹1.7 lakh at the top end. Part of that gap is genuine positioning, not just pricing strategy: Maruti sells the Fronx through its Nexa network, generally the more premium-feeling showroom experience, while the Brezza sits in the mainstream Arena network. If dealership experience or resale perception factors into your decision at all, that's worth knowing going in, independent of anything on the spec sheet. Both are exactly the same length, built to the same sub-4-metre tax bracket, but they don't feel the same in person. The Brezza is 135mm taller with a genuinely SUV-like upright stance and a commanding driving position, plus 20 litres more boot space. The Fronx trades that height for a coupe-styled crossover profile, a slightly longer wheelbase, and styling that reads younger. The Fronx's 8mm ground clearance deficit is minor on paper but matches its overall positioning, this is a crossover built on the Baleno platform, not a purpose-built SUV. If rear headroom and outright boot capacity matter more to you than styling, the Brezza's dimensions do the talking.

Engines & Transmissions: The Same Two Choices, Mirrored

Laid out side by side, the mirroring is obvious. Both offer a naturally aspirated engine and a turbo, and both restrict their genuine torque-converter automatic to just one of the two. The Brezza's non-turbo engine also has more displacement and torque than the Fronx's, which shows up in how relaxed each car feels off the line before you factor gearbox choice in at all. If you've already decided you want the turbo specifically for the performance and you also want a proper automatic rather than a manual or AMT, the Fronx is currently the only one of these two that can give you that combination. The Fronx's smaller-displacement engines claim a consistent efficiency edge across every powertrain, which tracks with its lower kerb weight and smaller engine capacities. It's not a dramatic gap in any single category, roughly 1 to 1.6 km/l or km/kg depending on what you're comparing, but it's a real and consistent one. Safety: Brezza Has a Published Rating, Fronx Doesn't Yet

This is a genuine, current gap, not a close call. The Brezza has a published 5-star Bharat NCAP rating, scoring 30.41/32 for adult occupant protection and 43/49 for child occupant protection. The Fronx has not been tested under Bharat NCAP as of 2026. You'll see an older 1-star Global NCAP figure attached to the Fronx in some coverage, but that predates the current car's standardised 6-airbag setup and shouldn't be read as describing today's India-spec model, it's an outdated number, not a current one. Both cars now come with 6 airbags, ABS with EBD, ESP, hill-hold assist, and ISOFIX mounts as standard. But until the Fronx gets a current Bharat NCAP result published, the Brezza is the only one of the two with an up-to-date, independently verified crash-test outcome to point to.

Features & Cabin: Close, With Different Priorities

Both top trims are well-equipped for the segment. The Brezza's flagship gets a 10.1-inch touchscreen, a 360-degree camera, ambient lighting, and wireless charging. The Fronx counters with its own 360-degree camera, a head-up display, and Suzuki Connect on its higher trims. Neither car meaningfully outclasses the other here, the practical difference is more about cabin theme: the Fronx leans into a sportier, dual-tone design aimed at younger buyers, while the Brezza's cabin reads a little more conventional and family-oriented. If a specific feature is a dealbreaker for you, check it against the exact trim you're planning to buy rather than the top-of-range spec sheet, feature availability varies meaningfully by trim on both cars.

Our Verdict

If you've already decided you want the turbo engine and a proper automatic together, this isn't really a comparison, the Fronx is your only option between these two, since the Brezza's turbo is manual-only. If you want a relaxed, non-turbo automatic instead, the reverse is true: the Brezza is your only option, since the Fronx's naturally aspirated engine doesn't get a true automatic. Outside of that gearbox-and-engine constraint, the Brezza wins on size, boot space, and its published 5-star safety rating, while the Fronx wins on price and claimed mileage across every powertrain. Pick the Brezza If

  • You want a relaxed, non-turbo automatic, the Fronx doesn't offer this pairing
  • You want the taller, more upright SUV stance and more boot space
  • A published, current 5-star Bharat NCAP rating matters to your decision
  • You want CNG on a bigger-displacement, higher-torque engine

Pick the Fronx If

  • You want the turbo engine paired with a proper torque-converter automatic
  • Lower entry and top-end pricing matters more to you than cabin space
  • You prefer the sportier, coupe-styled crossover look over a boxier SUV
  • Slightly higher claimed mileage across every powertrain is a priority

FAQs

Can I get the Brezza automatic with the turbo engine?
No. The Brezza's 6-speed torque-converter automatic is only available with the 1.5-litre naturally aspirated petrol. The 1.0-litre turbo is manual-only.

Can I get the Fronx automatic with the naturally aspirated engine?
No. The Fronx's real 6-speed torque-converter automatic is only available with the 1.0-litre turbo-petrol. The 1.2-litre naturally aspirated engine is offered with a manual or an AMT, not a true automatic.

Which is safer, Brezza or Fronx?
The Brezza has a published 5-star Bharat NCAP rating. The Fronx has not yet been tested under Bharat NCAP as of 2026; an older global-spec Fronx scored 1-star under Global NCAP before 6 airbags became standard, so that older figure shouldn't be read onto the current 6-airbag India-spec car.

Which has better mileage, Brezza or Fronx?
Powertrain for powertrain, the Fronx's smaller engines claim slightly higher ARAI figures across the board: its 1.2 NA petrol claims up to 22.89 km/l versus the Brezza 1.5's 21.09 km/l, and its CNG claims up to 28.51 km/kg versus the Brezza's 26.90 km/kg.
