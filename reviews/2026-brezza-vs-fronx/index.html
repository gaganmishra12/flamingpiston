2026 Maruti Brezza vs Fronx: Which One Should You Buy?

AI illustration: FlamingPiston
Gagan Mishra
Published Aug 18, 2026 · Updated Nov 23, 2026
6 min read
Category: Compact SUVs
Share

The Brezza and the Fronx are both Maruti compact SUVs at overlapping prices, but they suit different buyers. The Brezza is the bigger, taller car with a five-star crash rating; the Fronx is cheaper, more efficient on paper and the only one of the two with a turbo-petrol automatic. One fact decides it for many buyers: each car pairs its automatic with a different engine.

Quick verdict

Buy the Brezza for space, a taller SUV driving position, the five-star Bharat NCAP rating and a 1.5 automatic. Buy the Fronx for a lower price, better claimed mileage, a spare wheel and a turbo with an automatic.

The automatic decides it for many buyers

The Brezza’s six-speed torque-converter automatic comes only with its 1.5 petrol; its 1.0 turbo is manual-only. The Fronx does the opposite: its six-speed torque-converter automatic comes with the 1.0 turbo, while its 1.2 petrol is offered with a manual or a five-speed AMT. If you want a turbo with a conventional automatic, only the Fronx has it. If you want a non-turbo torque-converter automatic, only the Brezza has it.

The two are the same length, but the Brezza is 135 mm taller and 25 mm wider, which shows in its upright SUV stance and the extra 20 litres of boot. Autocar India rates the Brezza one of the few compact SUVs that seats three adults across the rear in comfort. The Fronx’s advantage is practical: it keeps a spare wheel, for both petrol and CNG, while no Brezza does. Maruti does not publish the Brezza’s ground clearance in its brochure; 198 mm is V3Cars’ figure.

Safety

Both have six airbags and ESP as standard. The Brezza has a five-star Bharat NCAP rating, with 30.41/32 for adults and 43/49 for children, which covers its petrol and turbo versions but not the CNG. As of 23 November 2026, the Fronx has not been rated by Bharat NCAP or Global NCAP. The Brezza has no ADAS on any version. The top Fronx Alpha and the Brezza ZXi+ both have a 360-degree camera and a head-up display. See our Brezza safety guide.

Which to buy

  • Buy the Brezza if you want a roomier, taller SUV, a crash rating, a non-turbo torque-converter automatic, or the bigger boot.
  • Buy the Fronx if you want to spend less, want a turbo automatic, want the best claimed mileage, or want a spare wheel.

For the full Brezza verdict, read our Brezza review, and for its closest Tata rival, Brezza vs Nexon.

What Owners Say

The facelifted Brezza went on sale in July 2026, so owner reports are still early and most cover under 2,000 km. On 23 November 2026 we read owner posts on Team-BHP, CarWale and CarDekho, leaving out test drives, posts about the older model, duplicate reviews and anyone who had not bought or driven the car. These are owners’ experiences, not measured results; we say how many owners each point rests on. For the Fronx, we read the owner reviews on CarWale; we did not find enough Fronx owners on other sites to compare.

  • Brezza: comfort and space. At least seven Brezza owners across CarWale, Team-BHP and CarDekho praise the comfort, and three call the boot practical. One owner on each of Team-BHP and CarWale names the missing spare wheel as the main drawback.
  • Fronx: comfortable and easy in town. Most of the seven Fronx owners we counted on CarWale call it comfortable and easy to drive in city traffic.
  • Fronx: mileage. One Fronx Alpha turbo owner reports 16–18 km/l in the city and about 22 km/l on the highway. This is one owner.
  • Fronx: features on mid trims. One Fronx owner wishes the popular Delta+ had more of the top trims’ features, such as the 360-degree camera.

Owners of both cars are broadly happy, and nothing they report changes our advice: the Brezza for space and its crash rating, the Fronx for price and a spare wheel.

FAQs

Which is cheaper, the Brezza or the Fronx?
The Fronx. It costs ₹6.95 lakh to about ₹12.08 lakh ex-showroom, against ₹7.55 lakh to ₹13.76 lakh for the Brezza (November 2026).

Which has a turbo automatic, the Brezza or the Fronx?
Only the Fronx. Its 1.0 turbo is offered with a six-speed torque-converter automatic. The Brezza’s 1.0 turbo is manual-only; its automatic comes with the 1.5 petrol.

Is the Brezza safer than the Fronx?
The Brezza has a five-star Bharat NCAP rating (30.41/32 adult, 43/49 child) for its petrol and turbo versions. The Fronx has not been rated by Bharat NCAP or Global NCAP. Both have six airbags and ESP as standard.

Which has more boot space?
The Brezza, with 328 litres against 308 litres. But the Fronx carries a full steel spare wheel in the boot, and no Brezza has a spare.

Which gives better mileage, the Brezza or the Fronx?
The Fronx, on claimed figures: up to 22.89 km/l for the 1.2 AMT and 28.51 km/kg for the CNG, against up to 21.09 km/l and 26.90 km/kg for the Brezza.
