Mercedes GLC Electric at ₹78.50 Lakh: 6 Catches to Know

Image: Mercedes-Benz India, edited by FlamingPiston
Gagan Mishra
Published Nov 20, 2026 · Updated Nov 21, 2026
7 min read Category: Electric SUV
Share f 𝕏

The Mercedes GLC Electric costs ₹78.50 lakh ex-showroom, only about ₹1.5 lakh more than the petrol GLC. For that you get a 94 kWh battery, around 490 hp and a claimed 711 km of range. On paper it is the easiest luxury EV in India to justify. The catches are in what the India-spec car leaves out, and how long you will wait.

A Note on This Review

This is a buyer’s brief, not a road test. It uses Mercedes-Benz India’s launch information as reported by Autocar India and Business Today; Mercedes-Benz India’s own site could not be read for this update. Checked on 21 November 2026. See our Review Methodology for how we separate editorial assessment from measured testing.

Quick Verdict

If you were going to buy a petrol or diesel GLC anyway and can charge at home, the electric one is the better buy for nearly the same money. Go in knowing three things. India’s car runs on passive dampers, not the air suspension and rear-wheel steering offered abroad. It has no ventilated seats. And Autocar India reports it is sold out until March 2027.

1

There is one version, the GLC 400 4MATIC with the AMG Line pack as standard, assembled at Mercedes’ Chakan plant near Pune. Autocar India puts the premium over the combustion GLC at just ₹1.5 lakh for the petrol and ₹50,000 for the diesel. Mercedes reported more than 1,000 bookings at launch, and Autocar India says the car is sold out until March 2027. If you book now, ask the dealer for the expected delivery month in writing, and whether a price rise would apply to your booking.

Mercedes also lets you choose between a free AC wall box and 2,500 kWh of public charging credits valid for 24 months, subject to its terms. At the claimed efficiency, 2,500 kWh is roughly 18,900 km of driving. Most owners who can charge at home should take the wall box.

2

Exterior Design

The GLC Electric is a new car on a new electric platform, not a converted petrol GLC. It is 140 mm longer, 23 mm wider and 12 mm taller than the combustion GLC, with an 84 mm longer wheelbase of 2,972 mm. The illuminated grille has 914 pixel elements that animate when the car is locked or unlocked, and the AMG Line pack brings 20-inch wheels and sportier bumpers as standard. The look is smooth and understated, without heavy cladding.

Image: Mercedes-Benz AG (international model shown)
3

Interior Design & Practicality

The dashboard is one continuous 39.1-inch Hyperscreen, the largest single screen fitted to any car in India. Autocar India found it slick and high-definition, but there are barely any physical controls, including for the air-conditioning. Trim choices are Black, Beech Brown or Ivory Beige, with open-pore wood and a 15-speaker Burmester system.

Practicality is a strength: a 570-litre boot plus a 128-litre front boot, and power-folding rear seats. The panoramic glass roof has no sunshade. Its opacity is controlled electronically, which is not the same as a blind that blocks heat completely. Sit in the car on a sunny afternoon before you decide.

Image: Mercedes-Benz AG (international model shown)
4

Features & Safety

The equipment list is long: nine airbags, Level 2 driver assistance using eight cameras and five radars, automatic parking, a 360-degree camera, two wireless chargers, a powered tailgate and ambient lighting. The notable omission is seat ventilation. The front seats heat and massage but do not cool, according to Autocar India. With a large glass roof, that is a real comfort loss in a hot Indian summer.

Image: Mercedes-Benz AG (international model shown)
5

6

Tech & Safety Deep-Dive

This section draws on Mercedes-Benz India’s launch information as reported by Autocar India and Business Today, not our own test, in line with our Review Methodology. The biggest catch is under the car. Abroad, the GLC Electric is offered with air suspension and rear-wheel steering. Autocar India reports that the India-spec car gets neither and rides on passive dampers, even in fully loaded AMG Line form. That matters most in a heavy EV on broken Indian roads, so book a test drive over your usual bad stretch, not just the dealer’s smooth loop. Mercedes’ launch information does not state a ground clearance for the India car, so try your building’s ramp before you buy.

The GLC Electric is not on Bharat NCAP’s ratings list, so its safety case in India rests on its nine airbags and driver-assistance suite.

Why These Scores

Design: An elegant, understated shape with a striking illuminated grille.

Performance: About 490 hp and a claimed 4.3-second 0–100 km/h.

Mileage Potential: A 711 km WLTP claim and 330 kW charging; untested by us.

Comfort: Passive dampers, no ventilated seats and a glass roof without a blind.

Features: The 39.1-inch Hyperscreen, Burmester audio and Level 2 ADAS.

Safety: Nine airbags and ADAS, but no Bharat NCAP rating.

Practicality: A 570-litre boot and 128-litre frunk in a bigger body than the petrol GLC.

Value for Money: Only ₹1.5 lakh more than the petrol GLC.

7

Verdict

At ₹1.5 lakh over the petrol GLC, the electric GLC is the version we would book, as long as you can charge at home. It is faster, roomier and better equipped, and much cheaper to run per kilometre in city use. Just don’t pay ₹78.50 lakh expecting the same car European reviewers drove: the India car is simpler underneath. If ride comfort on bad roads matters most, or you need the car before March 2027, wait or look elsewhere. The BMW iX3 is due next year.

Image: Mercedes-Benz AG (international model shown)

Who Should Buy This

  • Buyers who were already shopping for a petrol or diesel GLC
  • Anyone who can charge at home or at work
  • Drivers who do most of their driving in and around the city
  • Families who want a big boot plus a front boot

Who Should Avoid This

  • Buyers who put ride comfort on bad roads first
  • Anyone who cannot install a charger at home
  • Buyers who need the car before March 2027

FAQs

What is the Mercedes GLC Electric price in India?
₹78.50 lakh ex-showroom for the only version, the GLC 400 4MATIC. That is about ₹1.5 lakh more than the petrol GLC.

What is the GLC Electric’s range?
Mercedes claims up to 711 km (WLTP, ARAI-certified) from its 94 kWh battery. Expect less in real-world highway driving.

Does the India-spec GLC Electric get air suspension?
No. Autocar India reports the India car uses passive dampers and skips the air suspension and rear-wheel steering offered abroad.

What is the GLC Electric waiting period?
Autocar India reports it is sold out until March 2027. Ask your dealer for a delivery month in writing.

Does the GLC Electric have ventilated seats?
No. The front seats heat and massage but are not ventilated, according to Autocar India.

How fast does the GLC Electric charge?
Up to 330 kW on a DC fast charger, adding about 300 km in 10 minutes in ideal conditions.
