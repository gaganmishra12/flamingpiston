Mercedes GLC Electric at ₹78.50 Lakh: What You Get, and 6 Catches Before You Book

Image: Mercedes-Benz India, edited by FlamingPiston
Gagan Mishra
Published Nov 20, 2026
7 min read
Category: Electric SUV
Share

The Mercedes GLC Electric costs ₹78.50 lakh ex-showroom. That is only about ₹1.5 lakh more than the petrol GLC, for 360 kW, a 94 kWh battery and a claimed 711 km of range. On paper, that makes it the easiest luxury EV in India to justify. The catch is in what the India-spec car leaves out, and how long you will wait for one.

This is a buyer’s brief. We have not driven the GLC Electric. Everything here comes from Mercedes-Benz India’s launch release and published Indian reporting, listed at the end.

Quick Verdict

If you were going to buy a petrol or diesel GLC anyway and can charge at home, the electric one is the better buy for nearly the same money. Go in knowing three things. India’s car runs on steel springs, not the air suspension and rear-wheel steering the GLC Electric gets abroad. It skips the ventilated seats the petrol GLC has. And Autocar India reports it is sold out until March 2027.

What ₹78.50 Lakh Gets You

There is one version, the GLC 400 4MATIC, assembled at Mercedes’ Chakan plant near Pune. It is the first car Mercedes builds in India on its new MB.EA-M electric platform. Deliveries began in mid-November.

The 711 km figure is a lab number. In our guide to claimed vs real-world EV range, we explain why highway driving and air-conditioning load usually take a sizeable bite out of it. Plan your trips on a lower figure until owners report real numbers.

Electric vs Petrol GLC: The Price Gap Is Tiny

This is the GLC Electric’s strongest argument. Autocar India puts the premium at ₹1.5 lakh over the petrol GLC and ₹50,000 over the diesel.

For a buyer who drives mostly in the city and charges at home, the electric car is quicker, roomier inside and much cheaper to run per kilometre for nearly the same sticker price. The petrol car still makes sense if you cannot charge at home or you regularly drive long distances where fast chargers are scarce.

The Catches

1. No air suspension or rear-wheel steering

Abroad, the GLC Electric is offered with air suspension and up to 4.5 degrees of rear-wheel steering. Autocar India reports that the India-spec car gets neither and uses passive dampers instead, even though it is the fully loaded AMG Line version. That matters most in a heavy EV on broken Indian roads. Book a test drive over your usual bad stretch, not just the dealer’s smooth loop.

2. You will wait

Mercedes said it had more than 1,000 bookings at launch, and Autocar India reports the car is sold out until March 2027. If you book today, ask the dealer for the expected delivery month in writing, and whether a price rise in the meantime would apply to your booking.

3. No ventilated seats

The front seats heat and massage, but they do not cool, according to Autocar India’s launch report. The petrol GLC lists ventilated seats. With a large glass roof, that is a real comfort loss in a Delhi or Chennai summer.

4. The glass roof has no blind

Instead of a fabric sunshade, the roof darkens electronically in segments. It works, but it is not the same as a blind that blocks heat completely. Sit in the car on a sunny afternoon before you decide.

5. Wall box or charging credits, not both

Mercedes gives you a choice. Take the free AC wall box, or skip it and get 2,500 kWh of public charging credits instead. The credits last 24 months, are linked to the car’s VIN, and must be activated within 7 days of delivery. At the claimed efficiency, 2,500 kWh is roughly 18,900 km of driving, so the credits are worth more to someone who cannot install a home charger. Most owners who can charge at home should take the wall box.

6. Check the ground clearance yourself

Mercedes’ launch release does not state a ground clearance for the India car. evo India quotes 148 mm, compared with the 201 mm Autocar India lists for the petrol GLC. Reports also disagree on the suspension, so treat that number with caution. If your building has a steep ramp or you face big speed breakers every day, try them on the test drive.

What Else Can You Buy?

Right now, nothing quite matches it. The BMW iX3 long-wheelbase, its most direct rival, is due in India in 2027. Audi’s Q6 e-tron is also not on sale here yet, with Autocar India estimating a mid-2027 launch. Until then, the GLC Electric is the only electric version of a mid-size German luxury SUV you can book at this price.

Who Should Buy It

Buy It If

  • You were already shopping for a petrol or diesel GLC
  • You can charge at home or at work
  • Most of your driving is in and around the city

Wait or Look Elsewhere If

  • Ride comfort on bad roads is your top priority
  • You cannot install a charger at home
  • You need the car before March 2027

Our view: at ₹1.5 lakh over the petrol GLC, the electric GLC is the version we would book, as long as you can charge at home. Just do not pay ₹78.50 lakh expecting the same car European reviewers drove. The India car is simpler underneath.

FAQs

What is the Mercedes GLC Electric price in India?
₹78.50 lakh ex-showroom for the only version, the GLC 400 4MATIC. That is about ₹1.5 lakh more than the petrol GLC.

What is the GLC Electric’s range?
Mercedes claims up to 711 km (WLTP, ARAI) from its 94 kWh battery. Expect less in real-world highway driving.

Does the India-spec GLC Electric get air suspension?
No. Autocar India reports the India car uses passive dampers and skips the air suspension and rear-wheel steering offered abroad.

What is the GLC Electric waiting period?
Autocar India reports it is sold out until March 2027. Ask your dealer for a delivery month in writing.
