2026 Maruti Brezza Automatic Review: Is the Torque Converter AT Worth It?

Image: FlamingPiston
G
Gagan Mishra
Published Aug 17, 2026
6 min read Category: Compact SUVs

"Automatic" gets used loosely in this segment, covering everything from proper torque converters to notchy AMTs wearing a nicer badge. The Brezza's automatic is the real thing: a 6-speed torque-converter unit, the same broad category used in cars costing several times as much, not the clutch-based AMT that makes a lot of sub-₹10-lakh "automatics" feel like a compromise. But it only comes on one specific engine, at one specific price premium, and it drives with a personality that won't suit every kind of buyer. Here's what to actually expect.

A Note on This Guide

This guide draws on Maruti Suzuki's official specifications, published first-drive road tests, and owner-reported ownership accounts, not our own long-term test of the 2026 facelift. Where a claim comes from someone else's driving rather than a confirmed spec, we say so directly.

What "Automatic" Actually Means Here

The automatic is only available paired with the 1.5-litre naturally aspirated petrol engine. It is not offered with the new 1.0-litre turbo-petrol at any trim, and it's not offered on the base LXi either, VXi is the minimum trim you need. If either of those rules changes your shortlist, our Variants Explained guide covers every powertrain-and-trim combination in full.

The Gearbox Itself

It's a 6-speed torque-converter automatic, sourced from Aisin, with just two modes: Drive and a manual-shift mode operated through paddle shifters behind the steering wheel. There's no separate Sport mode, and no old-style '2' or 'L' gear positions, this is a simpler setup than some rivals offer, but also one with fewer things to get wrong. The 1.5-litre engine's Smart Hybrid mild-hybrid system is standard on this powertrain, and its ISG motor quietly assists the engine between roughly 1,400 and 2,000rpm under gentle throttle, before handing off entirely once you're past that band.

What It's Actually Like to Drive

Published first-drive reports consistently describe the same character: smooth, unhurried progress that feels most at home in stop-and-go city traffic, where the gearbox's job is mostly to be invisible. On the highway, road tests have clocked the automatic cruising at around 2,000rpm at 100kmph, a relaxed, low-stress state that keeps cabin noise down on long runs. The trade-off is the 1.5-litre engine's fairly modest 137Nm of torque, which several reviewers have flagged as adequate for daily driving but not eager for quick overtakes, expect to plan your gaps rather than expect instant urgency. The paddle shifters genuinely help here: multiple reviewers note they're responsive enough to hold a lower gear for engine braking on a descent, or grab an early upshift, giving you more control than a "set it and forget it" automatic usually allows.

What Owners Actually Report

The 2026 facelift is too new for meaningful long-term ownership data, so the real-world accounts below are from the outgoing, pre-facelift Brezza automatic, which used the same core 6-speed torque-converter unit. One Team-BHP owner, at roughly 24,000km over a year of ownership, describes the transmission as consistently smooth and specifically credits the paddle shifters for making quick overtakes easier, and explicitly compares it favourably against a previous car's jerky AMT. A separate CarWale long-term highway report recorded real-world fuel economy settling around 12.6kmpl after a mix of city and highway driving, well below the ARAI-claimed figure, which is normal for real-world numbers but worth knowing going in. For the full mileage picture, including what's changed with the 2026 facelift, see our Real-World Mileage guide.

Mileage: Claimed vs Real

Maruti's ARAI-certified figure for the 2026 facelift automatic is 20.17kmpl, against 20.81kmpl for the equivalent manual, a small, expected gap rather than a dealbreaker. As with any ARAI figure, treat it as a lab-tested ceiling rather than a daily-driving promise; our Real-World Mileage guide breaks down what owners are actually reporting by driving style and city versus highway use.

Who Should (and Shouldn't) Buy the Automatic

If most of your driving is city traffic and daily commuting, the automatic's relaxed, low-effort character is exactly what it's built for, and the torque-converter reliability track record (versus AMT alternatives in this price bracket) is a genuine point in its favour. If you regularly drive long highway stretches and value quick, decisive overtakes, the 137Nm engine will feel more limited here than in the manual, where you can hold a gear and extract everything the engine has yourself. Budget-focused buyers should also know the automatic effectively rules out the LXi and the entire CNG range, since neither is offered with this gearbox, a trade-off worth weighing against our Variants guide before you commit.

FAQs

Is the Brezza automatic a CVT or an AMT?
Neither. It's a genuine 6-speed torque-converter automatic, the same category of gearbox used in far pricier cars, not the jerky clutch-based AMT common in this price bracket.

Can I get the automatic with the turbo engine?
No. The automatic is only available on the 1.5-litre naturally aspirated petrol. The 1.0-litre turbo-petrol is manual-only across all four trims.

What is the cheapest Brezza automatic?
The VXi AT, priced at ₹10.61 lakh ex-showroom. The automatic isn't offered on the base LXi at all.

How much mileage does the Brezza automatic get?
Maruti's ARAI-claimed figure for the 2026 facelift automatic is 20.17kmpl. See our Real-World Mileage guide for what to actually expect day to day.
