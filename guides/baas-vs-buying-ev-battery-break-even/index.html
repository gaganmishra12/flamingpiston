EV Battery Rental (BaaS) vs Buying Outright: When Does Renting the Battery Cost More?

AI illustration: FlamingPiston
Gagan Mishra
Published Nov 18, 2026
4 min read
Category: Guide
Share

Battery-as-a-Service (BaaS) lets you buy an electric car without paying for its battery upfront. The showroom price drops by lakhs, and in return you pay a rent for every kilometre you drive. Whether that is a good deal depends on one number: how far you will drive before the rent adds up to the money you saved.

The Short Answer

Divide the upfront saving by the rent per kilometre. If you will drive fewer kilometres than that while you own the car, BaaS costs less on these numbers. For most of the EVs below, that point is well over one lakh kilometres. The real risks are rate increases, minimum monthly billing and unclear terms when you sell.

1

The Break-Even Formula

Break-even distance = (full price − BaaS price) ÷ battery rent per km

Example: if BaaS saves ₹2,00,000 upfront and the rent is ₹3.20/km, the break-even is 62,500 km. Drive less than that before you sell and BaaS was cheaper. Drive more and buying outright would have cost less.

Two things to remember. The rent is on top of your charging cost, which you pay either way. And the formula ignores interest: if you would have taken a loan for the extra battery money, buying outright costs more than the formula shows, which pushes the result further in BaaS’s favour.

2

In years: at 1,000 km a month, the Comet reaches break-even in a little over five years. The others take around ten years or more. At 1,500 km a month, most take about seven years, and the Syros EV about nine.

3

Minimum Billing Changes the Maths for Light Users

Some plans charge for a minimum distance each month even if you drive less. EV Select reports that the Maruti e Vitara bills a minimum of 1,800 km a month, about ₹7,182, and the Citroën eC3X a minimum of 2,000 km, about ₹4,520.

That matters if you drive little. On a 1,800 km minimum, someone who drives 800 km a month still pays for 1,800. Ask for the minimum in writing, and recalculate the break-even using the distance you will be billed for, not the distance you will drive.

4

Rates Have Gone Up Before

Per-km rates are not fixed for life on every plan. Car Quest India reports that the Comet’s rate was ₹2.50/km when BaaS was introduced in 2024 and has since risen to ₹3.20/km. AckoDrive reports the Windsor started at ₹3.50/km.

Before signing, ask whether your rate is locked for the contract, and how much notice you get of any change. A rise of ₹0.50/km on 1,500 km a month is ₹750 a month more.

5

Five Questions to Ask Before You Sign

  • Is the per-km rate fixed for the contract, and for how long?
  • Is there a minimum monthly distance or bill?
  • How long is the contract, and what happens when it ends?
  • If I sell the car, can the buyer take over the plan, and is there a fee?
  • Who covers the battery under warranty, and does BaaS change that cover?

If the answers are clear and you drive well under the break-even distance, BaaS is a sensible way to cut the upfront cost. If the answers are vague, the lower showroom price is not the whole story.

Charging cost is separate from all of this. For what range you can expect in daily use, see our claimed vs real-world EV range guide.

FAQs

What is BaaS for electric cars?
Battery-as-a-Service lets you buy an EV without paying for the battery upfront. You pay a lower showroom price plus a rent for each kilometre you drive.

How do I calculate BaaS break-even?
Divide the upfront saving (full price minus BaaS price) by the rent per kilometre. Below that distance, BaaS costs less; above it, buying outright costs less.

Is the battery rent in addition to charging cost?
Yes. You still pay for electricity. The per-km rent is an extra cost on top of charging.
