Kia Syros EV Review: Range, Price, Features & Should You Buy It (2026)

8.4 / 10

Published Aug 2026 · Updated Aug 2026
Reviewed by Editorial Team · Aug 2026 9 min read Category: Electric SUVs
Share f 𝕏

Kia's Syros EV enters the crowded compact electric SUV segment with a straightforward pitch: the longest certified range and the fastest charging in its class, wrapped in a heavily tech-loaded cabin. Prices start at ₹13,49,900 and run across seven trims, up to the range-topping X-Line ER. This review looks at what Kia is actually offering, and — more importantly — who it makes sense for.

Quick Verdict

A genuinely well-specified electric SUV on paper — strong range, fast charging, and a long battery warranty that removes a lot of the usual EV anxiety. It's a strong pick for tech-focused, city-based buyers, though this review is based on Kia's official specifications and has not yet been verified on a real-world test drive.

1

The Syros EV is offered across seven trims spanning two battery packs — a 42kWh unit good for a claimed 443km range, and a larger 51.4kWh Extended Range pack that pushes the certified figure up to 526km. Kia also offers a Battery-as-a-Service plan, unbundling the battery to bring the effective starting price down to around ₹7.99 lakh, with the battery billed separately on a per-kilometre basis.

Best Value Pick

The HTK+ is the sensible starting point rather than the base HTK. The entry trim is tempting on price, but it skips the rear camera and cruise control — features most buyers will expect as standard in 2026. Stepping up to HTK+ adds those essentials along with faster 100W charging, without committing to the larger, pricier 51.4kWh battery. The Extended Range packs are worth it only if the extra real-world range genuinely matters for your daily driving.

2

Exterior Design

The Syros EV wears Kia's "Digital Tiger Face" front end, paired with Ice Cube MFR LED headlamps and Star Map-pattern LED daytime running lights, giving it a distinctly futuristic look rather than a re-skinned combustion model. Around back, Starmap LED combination tail lamps, an integrated spoiler, and a shark-fin antenna carry the theme through. Details like flush-mounted "Auto Streamline" door handles, glossy black roof rails, and puddle lamps that project the Kia logo onto the ground add a level of visual polish Kia is clearly positioning above segment norms. Wheel sizes range from 16-inch on base trims up to 17-inch crystal-cut dual-tone alloys on higher variants.

3

Interior Design

Inside, the cabin is built around a 30-inch (76.2cm) Trinity panoramic display spanning instrument cluster, infotainment, and climate readouts into a single glass panel — a segment-first at this price point, per Kia. A floating centre console with a sliding cover, retractable cup holders, and open storage keeps the cabin looking clean. Higher trims add a dual-pane panoramic sunroof and 64-colour ambient lighting with LED footwell lamps (X-Line only). Kia claims best-in-segment second-row space, with a 60:40 split rear bench that slides and reclines, plus a 465-litre boot — usefully large for the class.

4

Features

Feature-specific photos coming soon — exterior shot shown as placeholder

The feature list is dense for the segment: an 8-speaker Harman Kardon sound system, wireless phone charging, ventilated front and rear seats, a 4-way power driver's seat, and a dual-camera dashcam with mobile app access. Connectivity runs through Kia Connect 2.0, offering over 95 features including OTA software updates, remote window control, scheduled charging, remote battery conditioning, multilingual voice commands, and smartwatch access. Digital Key — letting you lock, unlock, and start the car from your phone — is reserved for the top X-Line trim.

Safety kit includes 6 airbags, a 360-degree camera, electronic stability control, and hill-start assist as standard, with the range-topping ER trim adding ADAS Level 2 with 16 autonomous features. That list is genuinely comprehensive for the segment: forward collision warning and avoidance-assist covering cars, pedestrians, cyclists, and junction-turning scenarios; smart cruise control with stop-and-go; lane departure warning, lane keep assist, and lane follow assist; high beam assist; driver attention warning; reverse parking collision-avoidance assist; and a blind view monitor built directly into the instrument cluster. The Syros lineup is built on Kia's reinforced K1 platform, the same architecture underpinning the standard petrol/diesel Syros, which gives the EV version a strong structural baseline to inherit.

Elsewhere, ambient touches carry over from the wider Syros range too — 64-colour ambient mood lighting, a Harman Kardon 8-speaker system, and a fully automatic climate control panel on a dedicated 5-inch touchscreen.

On paper, the 171 PS output and 526 km range are both class-leading figures Kia is happy to lead with, and the 39-minute fast-charge time (10–80%) would make road trips considerably less stressful than most EVs in this price bracket. The lifetime high-voltage battery warranty is also a meaningful ownership reassurance, addressing one of the biggest hesitations first-time EV buyers have.

6

Ride & Handling

Ride & Handling photos coming soon — exterior shot shown as placeholder

This section is based on Kia's published engineering positioning rather than an in-person test drive — we'll update it once we've driven the car, in line with our Review Methodology. Electric SUVs in this weight and power class typically deliver strong low-speed response thanks to instant torque, which should make the Syros EV feel eager around town. Given the added weight of the battery pack, expect the ride to lean toward stability over outright agility — a sensible trade-off for a car aimed at daily commuting rather than back-road driving. We'd recommend a proper test drive to judge steering feel and body control for yourself before buying.

7

Verdict

The Syros EV's specification sheet is genuinely competitive — range, charging speed, and warranty coverage all lead or match the best in its class, and the feature list punches well above what most buyers would expect at this price. Kia is clearly trying to remove every common objection to buying an EV in one product. Whether it delivers on ride comfort and everyday usability the way the spec sheet promises is the one thing left to confirm on the road.

Who Should Buy This

  • First-time EV buyers who want the reassurance of a lifetime battery warranty and buyback programme
  • City-based drivers who want the segment's fastest charging for occasional longer trips
  • Tech-focused buyers who'll actually use a large display and a deep connected-app feature set
  • Buyers with reliable home or workplace charging access

Who Should Avoid This

  • Buyers who want proven long-term reliability data — this is a new-generation model without a track record yet
  • Anyone without consistent access to charging at home or work
  • Buyers who prioritize driving dynamics and outright performance over range and practicality
  • Budget-focused buyers — pricing sits closer to premium compact SUVs than entry-level EVs
