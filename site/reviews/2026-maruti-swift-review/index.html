Maruti Swift Review 2026: Mileage, AMT or CNG?

The Swift makes most sense when your car spends more time carrying one or two people through town than moving a family and holiday luggage. Its buying argument is straightforward: a hatchback with petrol, AMT and CNG choices, backed by published efficiency figures that deserve a closer look. The decision is less about which version has the longest feature list and more about the routine it will have to handle.

Specification-based buying review, checked 23 October 2026. We have not road-tested this vehicle for this article. Quoted figures are manufacturer information; recommendations are our editorial assessment.

Price: Get the Exact Variant on the Quote

At our 23 October check, Maruti’s main Arena navigation listed the Swift from ₹5,83,900, while some indexed official snippets still displayed different starting amounts. Use that figure as a reference, not a guaranteed transaction price. Ask for a dated quotation identifying the variant, fuel, transmission and any colour surcharge. Separate the car price from registration, insurance and optional accessories.

Our starting shortlist would be an AMT-equipped mid-range petrol variant for congested commuting, or a manual petrol if simplicity and a lower purchase bill matter more. CNG deserves a separate calculation; it is not automatically the cheapest option for someone driving only at weekends.

Petrol Manual, AMT or CNG?

An AMT automates clutch operation and gear selection, but the letters do not promise the same behaviour as every other automatic. On the test drive, concentrate on gentle acceleration, crawling traffic and the pause between gear changes. Try the parking manoeuvres you perform every day. A short burst on an empty road answers the wrong question.

Choose the manual if you are happy operating a clutch and want control over when the car shifts. For CNG, inspect the actual luggage space and check refuelling availability along your normal route. A distant station or regular queue is part of the ownership cost, even when it never appears on the fuel bill.

Mileage: Use the Claims Correctly

The efficiency figures in the table are manufacturer-published claims, not FlamingPiston test results. They are useful for comparing equivalent versions under the same certification basis. They cannot tell you the consumption of a short, air-conditioned commute in heavy traffic.

For a CNG calculation, divide local fuel prices by realistic efficiency estimates, then divide the additional purchase cost by the saving per kilometre. Keep kilometres per litre and kilometres per kilogram separate. Our Brezza petrol-versus-CNG guide explains the arithmetic; substitute Swift-specific quotations and assumptions.

Cabin and Safety: Bring Your Passengers

The Swift’s six standard airbags are a useful equipment point, but airbag count is not a crash-test rating. We are not assigning a safety score from the feature list. Check the exact equipment sheet and child-seat installation requirements.

Set the driver’s seat for your own height, then sit behind it. Try your regular luggage before choosing CNG. Check whether the rear vents, camera, smartphone connection and seat-folding arrangement you want are included in the quoted trim. Features pictured on a model page may belong to a more expensive version.

Strengths, Compromises and the Right Buyer

The strengths on paper are the choice of running-cost approaches and standard six-airbag equipment. The compromises to investigate are AMT shift behaviour, the CNG luggage arrangement and whether the rear seat suits your family. We have not measured cabin space or driven this car for this article.

If most trips involve a full rear bench and several bags, compare the Swift with a larger hatchback before paying. If your main priorities are an easy daily routine and controlled costs, spending on a suitable gearbox can be more useful than spending on cosmetic equipment.

Flaming Piston's Take

Start with the petrol AMT for a traffic-heavy commute and the manual petrol for a lower-cost, lower-mileage purchase. Choose CNG only after the saving and luggage checks work for you. The Swift is worth shortlisting as an everyday hatchback; the right version matters more than the badge on the boot.

FAQs

Is the Swift mileage figure a real-world result?
No. The figures quoted here are manufacturer claims, not our measured consumption.

Which Swift should a city buyer consider?
Our starting shortlist is a petrol AMT, subject to a test drive and the exact variant price.

Is CNG always cheaper overall?
No. The extra purchase cost, usage, efficiency and refuelling routine determine the result.
