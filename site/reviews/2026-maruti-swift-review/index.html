Desk-researched editorial review, checked 23 October 2026. FlamingPiston has not road-tested this vehicle for this article. Manufacturer claims and other publications’ driving findings are identified separately; the judgments and scores below are our editorial assessment.

The Swift still has the right outline for a small car with some personality. The harder question is what you expect that personality to deliver. Its strongest argument today is efficient everyday transport with a choice of petrol, AMT and CNG. Buy it expecting a miniature performance car and you are asking the wrong engine to do the wrong job.

Quick Verdict

The petrol Swift is the sensible starting point for a small household. The AMT earns its place in traffic; CNG earns its place only with enough kilometres to repay the extra cost. Rear-seat ride comfort and luggage space keep this from being an automatic recommendation for every family.

Best Value Pick

Our pick is a mid-range petrol AMT for a traffic-heavy commute. Spend on the gearbox before cosmetic upgrades. For light use, the petrol manual makes more sense; the CNG premium needs regular mileage and convenient refuelling to justify itself. The entry figure is Maruti's checked listing, not an AMT or CNG price.

2

Exterior Design

The Swift's appeal is proportion rather than decoration. The rounded roof and compact hatchback shape still give it a clear identity without needing SUV cladding to announce itself. To our eyes, that is more convincing than adding bulk simply to look expensive. It earns design credit for remaining recognisable; it does not earn extra cabin space for doing so.

3

Interior Design & Comfort

This is a cabin to judge as an everyday hatchback, not a miniature luxury car. Autocar India's 2024 road test described the rear bench as suitable for two adults and noted the petrol car's 265-litre boot and high loading lip. Those observations apply to the tested generation, not a fresh FlamingPiston measurement.

The resulting compromise is straightforward: the Swift suits a couple or a small family better than repeated five-person trips with luggage. CNG changes the cargo equation again. A cheaper kilometre is less impressive if the cylinder occupies the space your weekend bags need.

4

Features & Practicality

Rear air vents, automatic climate control, a reversing camera and wireless charging appear in the range, with availability depending on trim. Prioritise ventilation and parking assistance over showroom sparkle. Equipment at the top of the range should not be used to describe the entry car.

The Swift's standard six-airbag provision is the more substantial improvement in the buying case. It benefits the person choosing a cheaper version too, which matters more than an accessory list that only becomes useful after the budget has been stretched.

Performance & Mileage Potential

The 1,197 cc petrol engine's published 60 kW output sets expectations: this is an efficiency-led hatchback. The manual gives you control of the shift; the AMT removes clutch work but remains an automated manual, so it should not be confused with a conventional torque-converter automatic.

Autocar India's first drive praised the car's willingness to change direction but found the rear ride firmer than the front. That is why our performance assessment is positive without pretending the Swift has no comfort trade-off.

Maruti claims 24.8 km/l for the petrol manual, 25.75 km/l for the AMT and 32.85 km/kg for CNG. These are certification claims, not commuting results. They support a strong mileage-potential score; they do not establish the saving for a particular owner's traffic, fuel prices or annual distance.

6

Tech & Safety Deep-Dive

Six airbags, stability control, ABS with EBD and ISOFIX are meaningful equipment. Our safety assessment gives them credit without converting the feature count into a crash rating. We have not verified an applicable India-spec crash result for the reviewed configuration in this assessment.

That distinction matters when comparing it with another hatchback: equipment and demonstrated crash protection answer related but different questions. A camera helps with parking; it cannot answer the crash-protection question either.

Verdict

Flaming Piston's Take

The Swift works when you buy the car your routine needs. Petrol manual for a modest budget, AMT for frequent traffic, CNG for mileage that makes the numbers work. Its shape and manoeuvrable size remain appealing, but the rear-seat and luggage compromises deserve as much attention as the fuel claim. Our recommendation is strongest for the petrol city car, not for every version wearing the same badge.

Who Should Buy This

  • One- or two-person households wanting an efficient daily hatchback.
  • City drivers willing to accept AMT shift pauses for less clutch work.

Who Should Avoid This

  • Families regularly carrying three adults across the rear bench and full luggage.
  • Buyers expecting strong performance simply because it is a Swift.

FAQs

Is the Swift mileage figure a real-world result?
No. The figures quoted here are manufacturer claims, not our measured consumption.

Which Swift should a city buyer consider?
Our starting shortlist is a petrol AMT, subject to a test drive and the exact variant price.

Is CNG always cheaper overall?
No. The extra purchase cost, usage, efficiency and refuelling routine determine the result.
