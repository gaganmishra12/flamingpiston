The Seltos cannot win this argument on screen count alone. The 2026 car is bigger, and the range gives you enough engines and trims to turn a straightforward family-SUV purchase into homework. What matters is whether the added space, reported comfort improvements and safety evidence make a better family car. Those are much stronger reasons to buy it than choosing whichever showroom example has the largest roof opening.

Quick Verdict

The Seltos makes a convincing family-SUV case, particularly when its engine and trim are chosen together. Start in the middle of the range. The turbo-petrol is the performance choice; the most expensive version is not automatically the best family purchase.

Best Value Pick

HTK(O) is our starting point for a balanced family specification. Kia lists the panoramic roof from this trim, but the reason to stop here is to avoid drifting into higher-trim money without a clear need. These are trim-entry prices, not quotations for every automatic or diesel combination. Select the engine first and compare the corresponding configuration.

2

Exterior Design

The new Seltos is visually assertive, with a more upright face and pronounced lighting graphics. Our view is that the extra visual drama works better as a whole than as a checklist of individual details. It has presence, but buyers who liked the earlier car's restraint may find this one busier.

At a published 4,460 mm long and 1,830 mm wide, this is a substantial five-seat SUV. More size can help passenger packaging, but it also occupies more of a parking bay. Bigger is a useful improvement only when you use the extra room.

3

Interior Design & Comfort

Autocar India's 2026 first drive reports a cabin with good assembly quality and retained physical controls for common functions. It also notes that the steering wheel can obscure the small climate display. That is a useful reminder that a broad digital dashboard and effortless usability are not the same achievement.

The same review found the ride more accommodating without giving away body control. Those published findings support our comfort assessment. We have not driven this car ourselves, and the equipment and wheels of the actual version still matter.

4

Features & Practicality

Kia offers a long equipment ladder, including the panoramic roof and driver-assistance systems on applicable versions. There is a sensible family car inside that ladder; the challenge is stopping before optional luxuries become an excuse to overspend.

A feature should improve the journeys you actually make. For rear passengers, cabin space and ride matter throughout a trip. A roof is enjoyable, but it does not replace either. That is the distinction behind our mid-range recommendation and our more cautious value score for the range as a whole.

Performance & Mileage Potential

The range separates three jobs: a naturally aspirated 1.5 petrol with manual or IVT, a 1.5 turbo-petrol with iMT or DCT, and a 1.5 diesel with manual or automatic. An iMT is not the same transmission proposition as a DCT, so the presence of two pedals alone is not enough to understand the choice.

Autocar's first drive preferred the turbo-petrol's response and found the diesel less forceful. That supports choosing turbo-petrol for performance; it does not rule out diesel for a high-mileage buyer.

Its current model listing gives a claimed efficiency span of 15.6–19.4 km/l across configurations. That wide span is exactly why a single 'Seltos mileage' number is unhelpful. Our mileage score is deliberately moderate: fuel choice and usage matter, and no FlamingPiston consumption test has been conducted.

6

Tech & Safety Deep-Dive

The March 2026 Bharat NCAP entry records 31.70/32 for adult protection and 45/49 for child protection, with five-star ratings. This is evidence for the new Seltos, rather than an assumption carried over from its predecessor. The official applicability list governs which configurations the result covers.

Kia also advertises Level 2+ assistance on equipped variants. Assistance remains supervised driving, not permission to stop watching the road. The strong safety assessment rests primarily on published protection evidence, not the number of electronic features in a brochure.

7

Verdict

Flaming Piston's Take

The new Seltos earns a place on a family shortlist through its comfort case, powertrain choice and published crash performance. It becomes harder to justify when the trim ladder pushes the budget far beyond the car you originally needed. Start with HTK(O), choose the right engine, and make every move upwards answer a specific need. This is a stronger purchase when the family makes the specification decisions, not the showroom display.

Who Should Buy This

  • Families seeking a five-seat SUV with published crash-test evidence.
  • Buyers who want a choice between petrol, turbo-petrol and diesel.

Who Should Avoid This

  • Drivers needing a small footprint for tight urban parking.
  • Buyers stretching to the top trim mainly for display features.

FAQs

Is the Seltos a seven-seater?
No. Kia lists it as a five-seat SUV.

Does every Seltos have a panoramic sunroof?
No. Kia states availability from HTK(O); check the current variant sheet.

Which automatic should I compare first?
For mainly city use, start by assessing the petrol IVT, then compare other powertrains against your needs.
