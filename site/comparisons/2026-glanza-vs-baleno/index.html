2026 Toyota Glanza vs Maruti Baleno: Compare the Variant, Not Just the Badge

The Glanza’s October facelift gives Baleno shoppers a fresh alternative, but the first comparison most people make is also the least useful: cheapest Glanza versus cheapest Baleno. It tells you where each range begins. It does not tell you what an equally equipped automatic or CNG version will cost.

Our Starting Point

Choose fuel and gearbox first, then match the equipment, then compare the final quotations. If you reverse that order, it is easy to talk yourself into a more expensive car to get a feature you did not need.

Price: The Entry Gap Is Not a Universal Premium

Toyota announced the Glanza from ₹6.99 lakh. The Baleno’s published September full-price list begins at ₹5,98,900. That is an entry-price gap of ₹1,00,100, using those dated ex-showroom figures. It is not evidence that every equivalent Glanza costs that much more. [1] [2]

Prices above are dated published references, not a live dealer quote. Insurance, registration, paint choices and optional packages can change the amount you actually pay. Use the same insurance cover and remove optional accessories before judging the difference.

CNG AMT Buyers Should Start in the Middle

The official model pages confirm Glanza S/G CNG AMT and Baleno Delta/Zeta CNG AGS. AGS is Maruti’s naming for its automated manual offering. [3] [4] This means a CNG buyer should compare those middle trims directly, rather than using the top petrol car as the reference.

Write down three things you will use every day: perhaps rear AC vents, a reversing camera and a particular seat adjustment. Check each against the exact CNG trim. Then drive both on the same route. The lower quotation is only useful if the car also meets the requirements you started with.

Do Not Mix the ADAS Car with the CNG Car

Toyota lists its ADAS functions on V(O), while CNG is listed in S and G. [3] The Baleno also separates its CNG trims from the upper Alpha variants. [4] Do not assume that selecting a model name gives you access to every feature-and-fuel combination advertised for that range.

If assisted driving features are essential, build a petrol shortlist around them. If fuel cost is the priority, compare the CNG choices on the equipment they actually include. That is a more honest decision than calling either car “fully loaded” without naming the variant.

Which One Would We Shortlist?

Start with the Baleno if the lower published entry price is central to your budget, or you already have a convenient Nexa workshop and a competitive quotation. There is little value in paying more purely for a different badge when your actual requirements are already met.

Keep the Glanza in the final two if your nearby Toyota dealer provides a better ownership package, a more suitable delivery date or stronger service convenience. Put a rupee value on the actual benefits rather than treating brand reputation as a blank cheque.

We have not conducted a back-to-back drive of these facelifted cars. This is a specification and purchase-process comparison, not a claim that one rides better or is more reliable. For the freshest launch details, see the Glanza news report; for negotiation, use our festive discount checklist.

FAQs

Is the Glanza more expensive than the Baleno?
The dated entry prices show a higher Glanza starting price, but equivalent variants must be compared using current quotations.

Which cars offer CNG automatic?
Both do: Glanza S/G CNG AMT and Baleno Delta/Zeta CNG AGS are listed on their official model pages.

Is this a road-test comparison?
No. It compares published specifications and buying considerations; FlamingPiston has not performed a back-to-back drive of the facelifted pair.
