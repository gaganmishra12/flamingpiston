New Toyota Hilux Review: What's Changed, Price, Specs & Features (2026)

G
Gagan Mishra Published Aug 4, 2026 · Updated Aug 4, 2026 · 8 min read
8.5/ 10 Octane Score
Share: f 𝕏 ✉

Toyota has given the Hilux a design and feature update for the Indian market, refreshing its street presence while keeping the platform's core off-road engineering untouched. Built on Toyota's globally proven Hilux platform — a nameplate with over five decades of history and more than 20 million units sold worldwide — the new Hilux is positioned less as a work truck and more as a rugged lifestyle vehicle for buyers who want genuine off-road capability with SUV-like comfort.

Quick verdict

A genuinely capable off-roader with strong torque and a comprehensive safety kit, best suited to buyers who actually need serious 4x4 ability rather than just SUV looks. Rear drum brakes and a large turning radius hold it back for city use — see who should buy and who should avoid it below.

1

Price & Variants

Best value pick

The GX 4x4 AT is the one to buy. It costs just ₹1.7 lakh more than the base 4x2, but that difference buys you the low-range transfer case, electronic differential lock, and terrain modes — the actual hardware that makes a Hilux a Hilux. The range-topping VX adds leather and a bigger touchscreen for ₹3 lakh more, but none of that changes what the truck can actually do off-road. Skip the base 4x2 unless you genuinely never plan to leave paved roads.

2

Exterior Design

The revamp brings a bold new trapezoidal front grille with a chrome surround, a redesigned front bumper with piano black accents, updated LED lighting on higher trims, and a refreshed Super Chrome alloy wheel design. It's a visual refresh rather than a mechanical overhaul — the engine, chassis, and 4x4 hardware carry over from the previous generation.

3

Interior Design

Higher trims get leather upholstery, dual-zone automatic climate control with rear air vents, an 8-inch touchscreen supporting Android Auto and Apple CarPlay, a TFT instrument cluster, and a power-adjustable driver's seat. The base variant is more work-truck-focused with fabric seats and manual air conditioning.

4

Features

The Hilux comes with 7 airbags, vehicle stability control, hill-start assist, ABS, ISOFIX child seat mounts, and front seats designed to reduce whiplash injury in a rear collision. Buyers can choose between a 6-speed manual (with an intelligent manual transmission system for smoother clutch engagement) or a 6-speed automatic with sequential shift and a shift-lock function.

5

Performance

The Hilux is powered by a 2.8-litre four-cylinder diesel engine producing 204 PS. Torque output depends on the transmission: the manual variants deliver 420 Nm, while the automatic version steps up to a stronger 500 Nm. It comes with a proper 4WD system featuring a limited-slip differential, plus low-range gearing for serious off-road use — not just a cosmetic all-wheel-drive badge.

6

Ride & Handling

This is where the Hilux sets itself apart from most SUVs in its price bracket. It features a genuinely tough ladder frame chassis, high and low-range 4WD, hill descent control, active traction control, and an electronic differential lock — hardware usually found on dedicated off-roaders rather than lifestyle trucks. On-road, expect a firmer, more utilitarian ride than a car-based SUV, and a large turning radius that makes tight city manoeuvring less pleasant.

7

Verdict

The Toyota Hilux makes the most sense for buyers who genuinely need serious off-road capability — not just SUV styling. If your driving is mostly city commutes, a Fortuner or a mid-size SUV will likely suit you better and cost less to live with day to day.

Who should buy this

  • Buyers who genuinely need serious 4x4 off-road capability, not just rugged looks
  • Anyone regularly towing or carrying heavy loads, thanks to the strong torque and payload capacity
  • Long-distance travellers who'll benefit from the 80-litre fuel tank

Who should avoid this

  • Primarily city drivers — the large turning radius and firm ride are a real daily trade-off
  • Buyers wanting rear disc brakes as standard, given the rear drums on this size of vehicle
  • Budget-conscious buyers, since pricing sits above most lifestyle SUV alternatives
