Innova Hycross Facelift Launched: Which Variant to Buy Now, and Is Old Stock Worth It?

AI illustration: FlamingPiston (based on a Toyota Kirloskar Motor image of the current model)
Gagan Mishra
Published Nov 22, 2026 · Updated Nov 26, 2026
5 min read
Category: Guide
Share

Updated 26 November 2026: Toyota launched the facelifted Innova Hycross on 25 November. This guide was first published before the launch; it has been rewritten around Toyota’s confirmed prices and specifications.

Toyota Kirloskar Motor launched the facelifted Innova Hycross on 25 November 2026, and bookings opened on 26 November. The headline change is under the bonnet: every hybrid is now a flex-fuel hybrid that Toyota says can run on any ethanol blend from E20 to E85. Prices start at ₹19.59 lakh ex-showroom for the GX petrol, and the hybrid now starts at ₹19.99 lakh. Toyota has not announced a delivery date.

The Short Version

Hybrid buyers should buy the new car: the hybrid range now starts lower than before and every hybrid gets the update. Consider leftover pre-facelift hybrid stock only with a large written discount. The GX petrol is still the earlier design, so pick it only if you specifically want petrol.

New Innova Hycross Prices

These are Toyota’s launch prices, ex-showroom. Toyota’s release does not call them introductory, although CarDekho’s report does, so confirm the price on the day you book.

Toyota’s price list marks the G Hybrid as a “Speed Limiting Function Grade” and does not explain it further. Business Today and other reports say the limit is 80 km/h, which suits fleet and city use more than highway touring; Toyota’s release does not state the figure, so confirm it with the dealer before you choose this grade. According to Autocar India, the hybrid used to start with the VX at ₹26.76 lakh; the new GX Hybrid is ₹2.77 lakh cheaper than that.

What Changed

Engine. The hybrid pairs Toyota’s 2.0-litre flex-fuel engine with its self-charging strong hybrid system. Toyota quotes a combined 147 kW (197 hp), EV, Eco and Power drive modes, and a claimed 22.58 km/l on E20. Toyota says the flex-fuel engine and the hybrid drive unit are made in India. The GX petrol is unchanged; Autocar India lists it at 173 hp and 206 Nm with a CVT.

Design. A new front bumper and grille, double-stacked LED daytime running lights, redesigned projector LED headlamps and new double-stacked tail-lamps. The side profile is largely the same.

Cabin and features. Toyota lists a 12.3-inch touchscreen with wireless Android Auto and Apple CarPlay, a 12.3-inch driver display, powered Ottoman second-row seats, powered reclining rear seats, ventilated front seats, an 8-way powered driver’s seat with memory, dual-zone climate control, a 360-degree camera and a 9-speaker JBL system. Toyota’s release lists these for the range, not grade by grade, so check which ones your variant gets.

Safety. Six airbags, vehicle stability control, hill start assist, an electronic parking brake with auto hold, tyre pressure monitoring and Toyota Safety Sense 3.0 (radar cruise control, lane tracking assist, blind-spot monitor and automatic high beam). Toyota lists these for the range; reports say the full Safety Sense suite is on the top grades, so check your variant.

Warranty. Three years or 1,00,000 km, extendable to five years or 2,20,000 km, and eight years or 1,60,000 km on the hybrid battery.

Which One to Buy

  • Most hybrid buyers: the GX Hybrid. At ₹23.99 lakh it is the cheapest hybrid without the speed-limiting tag, and it costs less than the previous entry hybrid did. Ask the dealer for the GX Hybrid’s exact feature list, since Toyota has not published one by variant yet.
  • The G Hybrid, if its speed limit suits you. It is only ₹40,000 more than the GX petrol. It is reported to be limited to 80 km/h, so confirm the limit with the dealer before you treat it as the bargain of the range.
  • VX or ZX, for the comfort features. Buy one of these if you want the powered rear seats and the rest of the premium list; compare the two at the dealer, because the grade-wise split is not public yet.
  • The GX petrol, only if you want petrol. It is the earlier design, with the older styling. It now has the bigger driver display and 17-inch alloys, but none of the facelift’s changes.

Is Pre-Facelift Stock Worth It?

Some dealers may still have unsold pre-facelift hybrids. We found no reported discount on them. Because the new hybrid range now starts ₹2.77 lakh below the old entry hybrid, an old VX hybrid makes sense only if the dealer’s written price is well below the new GX Hybrid, and you accept the older design, the smaller screen and a car that will look older at resale.

Before you sign, ask: Is this the facelifted car or old stock? What is the manufacturing month? Is the price on the booking form the final ex-showroom price? When will the car be delivered?

The flex-fuel ability only helps if you can buy higher ethanol blends where you drive; it also runs on regular E20 petrol. FAQs

When was the Innova Hycross facelift launched, and at what price?
Toyota launched it on 25 November 2026. Ex-showroom prices run from ₹19.59 lakh for the GX petrol to ₹31.99 lakh for the ZX hybrid; the flex-fuel hybrid starts at ₹19.99 lakh for the G. Bookings opened on 26 November.

What does flex-fuel hybrid mean on the new Hycross?
Toyota says the strong hybrid can run on any ethanol-petrol blend from E20 to E85. Its claimed fuel efficiency is 22.58 km/l on E20. Toyota calls it India’s first Flex Hybrid Electric Vehicle.

Is the old Innova Hycross still on sale?
Only the GX petrol. Toyota says the earlier GX gasoline continues at ₹19.59 lakh (7-seat) and ₹19.64 lakh (8-seat), with a larger driver display and 17-inch alloys. Every hybrid in the new price list is the facelifted flex-fuel model.

When do deliveries of the new Hycross start?
Toyota has not announced a delivery date. Ask your dealer for a written delivery estimate when you book.
