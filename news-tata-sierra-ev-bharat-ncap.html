Tata Sierra EV Gets a 5-Star Bharat NCAP Rating, but the Driver’s Feet Score Only ‘Marginal’

AI illustration: FlamingPiston
Gagan Mishra
Published Nov 21, 2026
4 min read
Category: News
Share

The Tata Sierra EV has a five-star Bharat NCAP rating for both adult and child occupant protection. It scored 30.62 out of 32 for adults and 45 out of 49 for children, and the rating applies to every variant, from the 63 kWh Pure to the 75 kWh all-wheel-drive Empowered A. The full fact sheet shows one weak spot the stars do not: in the frontal crash test, protection for the driver’s feet was rated only “marginal”.

What the Five Stars Do and Don’t Tell You

The Sierra EV is a five-star car in every version, so you don’t need the top variant for the same crash structure. Its adult score is mid-pack among electric SUVs, and the driver’s feet were rated only marginal in the frontal test. That shouldn’t rule it out, but compare the full fact sheets, not just the stars.

Bharat NCAP tested the top Empowered A 75 with all-wheel drive, at a crash-test weight of 2,412 kg. The side impact was perfect, and every body region scored “good” in it. The child dummies, an 18-month-old and a three-year-old in rear-facing i-Size seats, scored full marks in both the front and side crashes. The four child points the Sierra EV lost came from the vehicle assessment, the part of the child score that is not a crash test.

The Weak Spot: The Driver’s Feet

All 1.38 adult points the Sierra EV dropped were lost in the frontal test. Bharat NCAP grades each body region on five levels: good, adequate, marginal, weak and poor. For both front occupants, the head, chest, pelvis and thighs were rated “good”, and the lower legs “adequate”. The driver’s feet were rated “marginal”, the middle grade.

That does not undo the five stars, and Bharat NCAP gives no reason for the grade. It is still worth knowing, because star ratings hide the detail. If you compare two five-star cars, the body-region diagram on page 3 of each fact sheet tells you more than the star count does.

The Sierra EV matches the best child score in this group but sits mid-table for adults, about half a point behind its own petrol and diesel sibling. Every car here has five stars, and gaps of a point or less should not decide your purchase. The VF6’s adult score is the one clear outlier. Which Variants the Rating Covers

The fact sheet applies the rating to the whole range. With the 63 kWh battery, that means the Pure, Pure S, Adventure and Empowered. With the 75 kWh battery, it covers the Adventure, Empowered and Empowered A with rear-wheel drive, plus the all-wheel-drive Empowered A that was tested. Versions with the ACFC charger option are included too. It covers cars built from VIN MAT879029TAF90381 onwards. Bharat NCAP lists the rating from November 2026; the fact sheet gives June 2026 as the month of assessment.

On the tested car, the driver and front passenger each get a frontal airbag and a side airbag covering the chest and pelvis, and curtain airbags run the length of the cabin. Bharat NCAP lists four items as standard on every variant: electronic stability control, the curtain airbags, pedestrian protection, and a seat-belt reminder at every seat. There are no knee airbags, and the rear belts have no pretensioners.

What to Check Before You Book

  • Will child seats go in the back? ISOFIX mounts are on the two outer rear seats only.
  • Can the front-passenger airbag be switched off? The fact sheet lists a manual cut-off switch as standard; ask the dealer to show you where it is.
  • What will I pay today? At launch on 30 June 2026, Tata’s introductory ex-showroom prices ran from ₹18.79 lakh (Pure 63) to ₹25.99 lakh (Empowered A 75 AWD), and the 7.2 kW home charger cost ₹49,000 extra. Get the current on-road price for your variant in writing.
  • Which range figure applies to you? Tata claims 665 km on the MIDC cycle for the 75 kWh rear-wheel-drive car; read why a claimed EV range rarely matches a road trip before you plan around it.

FAQs

What is the Tata Sierra EV Bharat NCAP rating?
Five stars for both adult and child occupant protection. It scored 30.62 out of 32 for adults and 45 out of 49 for children.

Does the five-star rating apply to the 63 kWh Sierra EV?
Yes. Bharat NCAP applies it to every listed variant, including the Pure, Pure S, Adventure and Empowered with the 63 kWh battery, and to the 75 kWh rear-wheel-drive and all-wheel-drive versions.

Where did the Sierra EV lose points in the crash test?
All 1.38 adult points were lost in the frontal test, where the driver’s feet were rated marginal and both front occupants’ lower legs adequate. The four child points were lost in the vehicle assessment.
