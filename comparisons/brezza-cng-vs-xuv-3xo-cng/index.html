Brezza CNG vs XUV 3XO CNG: Boot Space, Spare Wheel or Power?

Images: Maruti Suzuki (Brezza), Mahindra (XUV 3XO), edited by FlamingPiston
Gagan Mishra
Published Nov 16, 2026
7 min read
Category: Comparison
Share

At ₹9.99 lakh, the Brezza VXi CNG and the XUV 3XO MX Edge CNG cost exactly the same. The Brezza gives you a 328-litre boot and cheaper running; the XUV 3XO gives you a spare wheel and 200 Nm of torque. Higher up the range, the XUV 3XO AX5 CNG costs ₹40,900 less than the Brezza ZXi CNG.

How We Assessed This

We have not driven either car. This comparison uses Maruti Suzuki’s and Mahindra’s published specifications, current ex-showroom price lists, both Bharat NCAP results, and Autocar India’s back-to-back real-world mileage test. Every figure is linked in the Sources box at the end.

The Price Ladder: “Cheaper” Depends on the Trim

Most comparisons stop at the starting prices and conclude the Brezza is cheaper. That is true at the bottom and the top. In the middle, it isn’t.

Brezza prices are the list after Maruti’s October 2026 revision, which added ₹5,000 to the LXi and ZXi CNG and left the VXi unchanged. XUV 3XO prices are Mahindra’s October launch list. Both cars are manual-only in CNG form, with a six-speed gearbox.

What that means: if your budget is ₹9.40 lakh, only the Brezza fits. At ₹9.99 lakh you are choosing a trade-off, not a price. And if you were heading for a Brezza ZXi CNG, the XUV 3XO AX5 CNG is worth seeing first, because it costs less.

Boot Space vs Spare Wheel: Each Car Gives Up One

Both makers moved the gas tanks out of the boot. They did it in different ways, and each car paid a different price.

Brezza CNGXUV 3XO CNG
Boot space328 litres (same as the petrol Brezza)270 litres (petrol XUV 3XO: 364)
CNG tanksTwin cylinders under the carTwin cylinders under the boot floor
Spare wheelNone; puncture repair kit and inflatorYes, mounted under the car

The Brezza keeps its whole boot, and the XUV 3XO CNG has 58 litres less, about 18% smaller. If your weekly load is a pram, a cabin bag and shopping, take them to both showrooms and try it with the rear seats up. Litres on a spec sheet won’t tell you whether your particular bags fit.

The cost of the Brezza’s layout is the spare wheel. No 2026 Brezza variant carries one, CNG or petrol. A repair kit seals a nail puncture; it can’t fix a torn sidewall or a burst tyre. That matters most on long highway runs and on roads where a tyre shop may be hours away. Our Brezza boot-space guide covers the loading area in more detail.

Running Cost: The Brezza’s Real Advantage

On paper the gap is small: Maruti claims 26.9 km/kg and Mahindra 24.7 km/kg. On the road it was bigger. In Autocar India’s back-to-back test, the Brezza covered 3.65 km more per kg in the city, 3.21 km more on the highway and 3.43 km more overall.

At Delhi’s CNG price of ₹86.98/kg (IGL’s rate since 29 August 2026), the claimed figures work out to ₹3.23 per km for the Brezza and ₹3.52 for the XUV 3XO. Real-world efficiency is lower than the claim for both cars, and the gap per km grows as efficiency falls. Here is a worked example:

At 1,500 km a month, that’s roughly ₹13,000 a year in Delhi.

Power: Where the XUV 3XO Pulls Ahead

In CNG modeBrezza CNGXUV 3XO CNG
Engine1.5-litre, 4-cylinder, non-turbo1.2-litre, 3-cylinder, turbo
Power88 hp111 hp
Torque121.5 Nm200 Nm

The XUV 3XO has 23 hp and 78.5 Nm more, and Mahindra quotes the same output in petrol and CNG modes. On paper that torque gap, about 65%, is what you would notice with five people aboard, on a ghat road, or when overtaking on the highway. We haven’t driven either car back to back, so treat this as a spec-sheet observation, not a driving verdict. Most CNG engines feel flat when fully loaded, and this is the clearest on-paper case against that.

One more practical difference: the XUV 3XO’s wheelbase is 2,600 mm against the Brezza’s 2,500 mm, which usually means more rear legroom.

Safety: Both 5 Stars, Rated Differently

What was testedZXi+ 1.5 automatic (petrol); rating applies to all variantsFresh frontal test on the AX5 CNG; side and pole results carried over from 2024

Two details are worth knowing. The Brezza’s score comes from a petrol automatic, and Bharat NCAP applies it to every variant, the CNG ones included. The XUV 3XO had a new frontal crash test as a CNG car. And the XUV 3XO’s two-point lead in child protection comes from a higher labelling-and-markings score (9 of 13, up from 7). Its child crash-test and child-seat installation scores are unchanged from 2024. In the adult score, the Brezza is about a point ahead. Our Brezza safety guide explains what each part of the score measures.

Which One to Buy

Buy the Brezza VXi CNG if you drive mostly in the city, cover well over 1,000 km a month and need the whole boot. It costs the same as the cheapest XUV 3XO CNG, and its running-cost advantage grows every month you own it. Ask the dealer for a quotation with a spare wheel added as an accessory, if one is available, and decide whether it’s worth it to you.

Buy the XUV 3XO CNG if your car is often full of people or does regular highway trips, where a spare wheel and 200 Nm count for more than 58 litres of boot. Look at the AX5 CNG first: it comes in under the Brezza ZXi CNG on price.

Our pick: the Brezza VXi CNG for most CNG buyers, because CNG buyers are buying lower running costs and the Brezza delivers more of them. If you were considering a Brezza ZXi CNG, compare it with the XUV 3XO AX5 CNG before you book.

Check each trim’s equipment against the current brochure before booking, because CNG trims don’t always carry the same kit as petrol ones. For the full Brezza range, see which Brezza variants you can actually order, and our Brezza CNG ownership guide.

FAQs

Which is cheaper, the Brezza CNG or the XUV 3XO CNG?
It depends on the trim. The Brezza LXi CNG (₹9.40 lakh) is the cheapest way into either car. The Brezza VXi CNG and XUV 3XO MX Edge CNG both cost ₹9.99 lakh. Higher up, the XUV 3XO AX5 CNG (₹11.19 lakh) is ₹40,900 cheaper than the Brezza ZXi CNG (₹11.60 lakh). All prices ex-showroom.

Which gives better mileage on CNG?
The Brezza. Its claimed figure is 26.9 km/kg against 24.7 km/kg for the XUV 3XO, and in Autocar India’s real-world test the Brezza covered 3.43 km more per kg overall.

Does the Brezza CNG have a spare wheel?
No. The 2026 Brezza comes with a puncture repair kit and tyre inflator instead of a spare tyre. The XUV 3XO CNG carries a spare wheel mounted under the car.

Are both rated 5 stars by Bharat NCAP?
Yes. The Brezza scored 30.41/32 for adults and 43/49 for children. The XUV 3XO CNG scored 29.36/32 and 45/49.
